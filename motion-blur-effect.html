Motion blur effect on iPhone photos

Last updated: September 2026

A motion blur effect on a still photo is directional softness—streaks that imply speed. It is not the same as gaussian haze, and it is not video. People search it for action shots, sports, and stylized social frames.

A motion blur effect smears pixels along a direction to suggest movement. In How to Blur Photo, you apply that look after capture, typically on the background so the subject still reads as the anchor.

When motion beat gaussian

Gaussian is even and calm. Motion has a heading: left-right for a runner, mild for a cyclist, stronger for a night street. If the photo is a seated portrait, gaussian or bokeh-style looks are usually more honest than fake speed.

The App Store listing calls out action shots as a use case. Match the effect to the picture you have, not to the keyword alone.

How to do it in the app

1

Pick an action still

A photo with implied movement takes motion blur better than a posed headshot.

2

Keep the subject

Detection should hold the person or vehicle. Motion on the subject itself can look like a mistake unless that is the intent.

3

Apply motion blur and save

Use the motion style from How to Blur Photo, preview at 100%, then save on-device.

Frequently asked questions

Is this for video?

No. How to Blur Photo is a photo editor. For clips, use a video app. This page is the still-photo motion blur effect.

Can I add motion blur after shooting?

Yes. That is the listing’s workflow: select a photo, adjust, save—no need to pan the camera at capture.
