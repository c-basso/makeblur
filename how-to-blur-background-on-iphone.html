How to blur background on iPhone after you already took the photo

Last updated: September 2026

Searching how to blur background on iPhone usually leads to Portrait mode. That path only works at capture time, on supported cameras, and only if you remembered to turn it on. If the photo is already in Recents, you need an editor that can blur the background after you shoot.

How to blur background on iPhone after capture means keeping the subject sharp while softening everything behind it on a photo that already exists. How to Blur Photo (Make Blur) is an iPhone app for that job: subject detection, intensity control, and on-device processing on iOS 17.1 or later.

Why Portrait mode is not the same job

iPhone Portrait mode records depth when you press the shutter. If the shot was taken in Photo mode, or the person stepped out of the depth range, Photos has nothing to refocus. Markup can scribble over a background, but that is not a photographic blur.

A dedicated blur app fills that gap: you open the existing image, the subject is separated, and you choose how strong the background goes. That is the search intent behind how to blur background on iPhone for most people who land here from Google—not a camera tutorial.

What you need

An iPhone running iOS 17.1 or later, the How to Blur Photo app (free to download; advanced styles are optional in-app purchases), and a photo in Recents. No internet is required to process the blur—the listing states images stay on the device.

Quick answers

Can I blur background on iPhone without Portrait mode?

Yes. How to Blur Photo works on photos you already took, including shots that were never captured in Portrait. That is the point of after-the-fact background blur.

Does this work on older iPhones?

The listing requires iOS 17.1 or later. Check Settings → General → About for your iOS version before you install.

Is a DSLR required?

No. The store copy is explicit: use it after you shoot, without a DSLR or desktop suite.
