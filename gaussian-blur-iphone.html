Gaussian blur on iPhone photos

Last updated: September 2026

Gaussian blur is the even, bell-curve softness used in almost every desktop editor. On iPhone, people want that same look on a photo they already took—not a 17-slider pro app.

Gaussian blur spreads pixel values with a smooth falloff. In How to Blur Photo it is the style for a calm, soft background while the detected subject stays sharp. Processing stays on the device.

Gaussian vs motion vs pixel

Gaussian: even, photographic, good for portraits and products. Motion: directional, for action. Pixelate/mosaic: privacy or graphic looks, not a lens simulation. Crystalize and ghosting are creative, not a stand-in for a fast prime.

If you searched only “gaussian blur,” start here, then branch to motion or face blur if that was the real job.

Frequently asked questions

Is gaussian blur the same as Portrait mode?

No. Portrait uses depth at capture. Gaussian blur here is an after-the-fact filter on a normal photo.

Can I use gaussian blur for privacy?

You can soften a region, but faces are clearer with the dedicated face-blur flow. See the face guide.
